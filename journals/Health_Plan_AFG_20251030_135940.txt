Typhoid:

   Injection:
   • Dose 1: Today or as soon as possible
     Note: Ideally, the typhoid vaccine should be given at least 1 month before you travel, but it can be given closer to your travel date if necessary. Booster vaccinations are recommended every 3 years if you continue to be at risk of infection.

   Oral Capsules:
   • Dose 1: Today or as soon as possible
   • Dose 2: 2 days after Dose 1
   • Dose 3: 4 days after Dose 1
     Note: Oral doses must be kept in refrigerator. Complete all doses at least one week before potential exposure.


Hepatitis A:
   • Dose 1: Today or as soon as possible
   • Dose 2: 6-12 months after Dose 1
     Note: Dose 2 can be administered after travel.


Polio:
   • Dose 1: Today or as soon as possible
     Note: The U.S. CDC is recommending adults who have been fully immunized as children get a single life-time booster as an adult before traveling to many global destinations. Additional doses of the vaccination are given in a single 3-in-1 Td/IPV (tetanus, diphtheria and polio) injection.


Rabies:
   • Dose 1: Today or as soon as possible
   • Dose 2: 7 days after Dose 1
   • Dose 3: 21 days after Dose 1
     Note: High risk country. Vaccine recommended for long-term travelers and those who may come in contact with animals.

   ⚠️  Warning: Dose 1 falls between 30 Oct 2025 and 31 Oct 2025 or between day 0 and day 1. Contact clinic for doses that MUST be given before/during travel.


Tetanus, Diphtheria, Pertussis:
   • Dose 1: Today or as soon as possible
     Note: Tetanus should be updated every 10 years especially if you're traveling.


Measles, Mumps, Rubella (MMR):
   • Dose 1: Today or as soon as possible
   • Dose 2: Given when a child is 3 years and 4 months old
     Note: You should make sure you and your children are up-to-date with routine vaccinations, including MMR, before travelling. If you haven't been fully vaccinated against these conditions or you're not already immune, you should ask about MMR vaccination before you travel. The MMR vaccine is given as 2 injections. These are usually given when a child is 3 years and 4 months old.
